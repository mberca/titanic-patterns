01 / Describe before predicting

A rate needs
a denominator.

Group averages can conceal both differences and uncertainty. These are sex-by-class summaries of the observed 891-row sample, separate from model evaluation. Every dot has a count and a Bayesian interval.

Dots: posterior means. Lines: central 95% credible intervals under a Beta(1,1) prior. Hover for observed counts.

What the sample shows

Intervals summarize uncertainty within the model. They do not correct incomplete records, historical selection, or the absence of the rest of the manifest.

The source’s recorded sex labels are female/male. These summaries describe recorded categories; they do not explain individual outcomes.

03 / Predictive comparison

More than
an accuracy score.

A prevalence baseline, random forest, and Bayesian logistic model share the same split. Shared ticket groups stay together. Imputation and scaling are learned inside each training fold—not from held-out rows.

The held-out comparison

CV: five training-only grouped folds; SD is fold-to-fold variability, not a 95% interval. Test: 179 rows. Balanced accuracy uses a fixed 0.5 threshold. A small holdout does not establish a universal winner.

Can the model rank outcomes?

Do probabilities match frequencies?

AUC measures ranking. Brier score and log loss measure probability quality. Calibration points can be noisy when bins contain few records. These views answer different questions.
04 / Bayesian logistic regression

Show the estimate.
Show its uncertainty.

Weakly informative normal priors regularize the coefficients. A Laplace approximation describes uncertainty around the posterior mode. These are conditional associations—not the effects of changing a passenger characteristic.

Conditional odds ratios

Log scale. Vertical line: odds ratio 1. Continuous terms are per training standard deviation. Intercept omitted.

What “Bayesian” means here

  1. A prior: independent Normal(0, 2.5²) coefficient priors and Normal(0, 5²) for the intercept.
  2. An approximation: posterior mode plus inverse-Hessian covariance. Intervals are marginal, approximate 95% credible intervals—not MCMC output.
  3. A prediction convention: comparison scores use posterior-mode plug-in probabilities, not a fully integrated posterior predictive.
  4. A limit: intervals depend on the likelihood, features, priors, and approximation. They are not causal or population guarantees.

Repository & methodology ↗
Data provenance, safeguards, and what this study cannot tell us

Public, historical data. The 891-row labeled sample is distributed by Paul Hendricks’ titanic package, whose source declares CC0. The revision and checksum are pinned. Names, cabin strings, and ticket strings are not committed. Data provenance ↗

Leakage safeguards. Shared tickets stay within groups. IDs and grouping keys never enter the predictors. Imputation, centering, and scaling use training data only, including inside validation folds. Hyperparameters and priors are fixed before looking at test results.

Selection and scope. This is not the complete ship manifest, nor a random sample for a modern application. Missing ages and historical recording choices constrain interpretation. Ticket groups do not perfectly identify families.

Descriptive, not prescriptive. Sex, class, and other observed characteristics are correlated with many unmeasured historical circumstances. No causal effect, rescue recommendation, fairness guarantee, or statistically significant model superiority is asserted.
